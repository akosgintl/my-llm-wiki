# Local PDF→Markdown & Document Parsing: State of the Art (August 2026)

## TL;DR
- **The center of gravity has shifted from CPU pipelines to small, self-hostable vision-language models (VLMs).** For a self-hosted IDP pipeline in mid-2026, the strongest all-round local choices are **MinerU 2.5 (1.2B)**, **PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B, Apache-2.0)**, and **dots.ocr (1.7B)** for VLM-grade fidelity, with **Docling** as the best MIT-licensed orchestration framework and **marker/Chandra 2** (Datalab) leading English olmOCR-bench when weights licensing permits.
- **"liteparse" is a real tool: `run-llama/liteparse`** — the open-source (Apache-2.0), Rust-based local parsing engine behind LlamaParse. It is a fast, layout-aware *heuristic* text extractor (no VLM), not a high-fidelity table/formula parser; it is the wrong tool for scanned or complex documents but excellent for born-digital throughput.
- **Pick by scenario:** CPU-only → pymupdf4llm (digital) or Docling classic pipeline; single consumer GPU → MinerU 2.5 or PaddleOCR-VL; highest fidelity on tables/formulas → PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 / MinerU2.5-Pro / GLM-OCR (all ≥94% OmniDocBench v1.5–1.6); high-throughput batch → olmOCR 2 or marker on GPU, liteparse/pymupdf4llm on CPU.

## Key Findings

1. **Two benchmark families dominate and both are near-saturation.** OmniDocBench (CVPR 2025; v1.5/v1.6) measures edit-distance/TEDS/CDM on English+Chinese; olmOCR-Bench (Ai2) is, per its dataset card, "a dataset of 1,403 PDF files, plus 7,010 unit test cases that capture properties of the output that a good OCR system should have," measuring pass/fail on (predominantly English) documents. Top OmniDocBench v1.6 scores now exceed 95% (MinerU2.5-Pro 95.69, GLM-OCR 95.15, PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 94.87). On the Nanonets IDP olmOCR-bench leaderboard, **Nanonets OCR-3 leads at 87.4%**, ahead of Datalab Marker (83.2) and Nanonets OCR2+ (82.0) — though as of May 2026 Unsiloed AI publicly claims #1 at 88.0 on the same pass-rate. LlamaIndex has publicly called OmniDocBench "saturated."

2. **Docling (IBM) is the leading open orchestration framework, now a Linux Foundation project.** Latest `docling` is **2.121.0 (Aug 20, 2026)**; `docling-ibm-models` is **3.14.0 (Aug 11, 2026)**. Its native models are the TableFormer table-structure model (FAST/ACCURATE modes) and a layout model; it added a single-pass **VLM pipeline** (`--pipeline vlm`) built on **Granite-Docling-258M** (released **September 17, 2025**, Apache-2.0). Docling was donated to the **LF AI & Data Foundation in April 2025**.

3. **Small specialized VLMs beat giant general models on document parsing.** A 0.9B PaddleOCR-VL and 1.2B MinerU 2.5 both surpass Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL-72B on OmniDocBench. This is the defining 2025–2026 trend: sub-2B document-specialized VLMs are now SOTA and run on a single consumer GPU.

4. **Licensing is the biggest practical differentiator.** MIT (Docling), Apache-2.0 (PaddleOCR-VL, liteparse, MinerU code, olmOCR, dots.ocr, Granite-Docling) are clean for commercial use; marker/Chandra weights use a modified OpenRAIL-M with a <$2M revenue/funding cap; pymupdf4llm inherits **AGPL-3.0**; MinerU adds commercial thresholds above very large usage.

## Details

### Docling (IBM) — in depth
Docling is a document-understanding *framework*, not a single model. Its classic PDF pipeline parses the PDF backend (text tokens + rendered bitmap), runs a layout model, feeds detected tables to **TableFormer**, matches predictions back to PDF cells (language-agnostic, avoids re-transcription), and assembles a structured `DoclingDocument` exportable to Markdown/HTML/JSON. It handles PDF, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, HTML, EPUB, images and audio (ASR pipeline), and integrates with LangChain/LlamaIndex.

- **Versions (Aug 2026):** `docling` 2.121.0; `docling-ibm-models` 3.14.0; supporting packages include docling-serve (Docker/CUDA images), docling-jobkit (Ray/RQ distributed jobs), docling-mcp (agentic/MCP), docling-graph (knowledge graphs), docling-eval.
- **Granite-Docling-258M** (Sept 17, 2025; Apache-2.0): per IBM's announcement, a "product-ready evolution of the experimental SmolDocling-256M-preview model released… in March 2025," replacing the SmolLM-2 backbone with a **Granite 3-based** architecture (Granite 165M LLM) and swapping the SigLIP encoder for **SigLIP2** (siglip2-base-patch16-512), on an Idefics3-derived architecture. Outputs IBM's **DocTags** markup. New features vs SmolDocling: enhanced equation recognition, flexible inference modes (full-page, bbox-guided region), improved stability (fewer infinite token-repetition loops). Backends: transformers, vLLM, ONNX, and MLX (Apple Silicon). Multilingual (English primary; experimental Chinese, Japanese, Arabic).
- **Granite-Docling reported metrics** (HF model card, vs SmolDocling): Layout F1 0.86; full-page OCR F1 0.84 / edit-distance 0.45; **Code** F1 0.988 (edit 0.013); **Equation** F1 0.968 (edit 0.073); **Table TEDS-structure 0.97**, TEDS-with-content 0.96 (FinTabNet 150dpi); OCRBench 500; MMStar 0.30. IBM trained the vision model on 81,000 manually labeled pages (patents, manuals, 10-K filings). IBM's Peter Staar — Principal Research Staff Member at IBM Research Zurich and Chair of the Docling Technical Steering Committee at the Linux Foundation — states that avoiding OCR "reduces errors, and it also speeds up the time-to-solution by 30 times."
- **Strengths:** MIT-licensed framework + Apache-2.0 model; best-in-class structured document object model; excellent table structure via TableFormer; broad format coverage; strong enterprise/RAG integration; Red Hat OpenShift Operator and RHEL AI / OpenShift AI integration; LF governance for long-term stability.
- **Weaknesses:** The **classic Docling pipeline scores poorly on OmniDocBench** — in the dots.ocr paper's Table 1 it posts an overall edit distance of **0.589 (EN) / 0.909 (ZH)**, with near-total failure on formulas (≈0.999–1.000 edit) and weak Chinese, versus MinerU's 0.150/0.357. Some of this reflects OmniDocBench's formatting-sensitive edit-distance metric, but the gap on formulas and CJK is real. The Granite-Docling VLM path narrows this, but Granite-Docling itself is a 258M model whose absolute accuracy on hard tables/formulas trails the 1–2B specialized VLMs.

### "liteparse" disambiguation
The user's "liteparse" is a real, recently-released tool: **`run-llama/liteparse`** (PyPI `liteparse`; npm `@llamaindex/liteparse`), described by LlamaIndex as "the core processing engine behind LlamaParse," now open-sourced (Apache-2.0). It is written in **Rust** (with Python/PyO3, Node/napi-rs, and WASM bindings), uses a custom **PDFium** fork plus a grid-projection algorithm, runs **entirely locally with no LLMs/API keys**, and can emit Markdown, JSON, or text with bounding boxes. OCR is available via Tesseract. Benchmarks (LlamaIndex, "Markdown Comes to LiteParse"): opendataloader-bench 0.875, **olmOCR-bench 0.391**, ParseBench 0.3279 — i.e., top scores among *model-free/heuristic* tools but far below VLM parsers. With OCR off it hits ~1,721 pages/sec but "collapses on non-linear layouts since it has no layout model." LlamaIndex's own positioning: "If you're building an agent…that needs to quickly read a PDF and move on, use LiteParse. If you're building a document intelligence product that needs to nail every table, use LlamaParse." **Verdict:** liteparse is the correct name and a legitimate fast local extractor — but it is a peer of pymupdf4llm, not of Docling/MinerU/marker. For complex IDP it should be a fast-path tier, not the primary parser.

### Other SOTA local tools & models

**MinerU 2.5 (OpenDataLab)** — Released Sept 2025 (arXiv 2509.22186). A 1.2B decoupled VLM. **OmniDocBench overall 90.67**, best-in-class text edit 0.047, formula CDM 88.46, table TEDS 88.22, reading order 0.044; olmOCR-bench 75.2 (a later MinerU2.5 build is cited at 77.5). Throughput ~2.12 pages/s (A100-80G), 1.70 pages/s (RTX 4090), 4.47 pages/s (H200). vLLM-based inference; 109 languages; VLM + classic pipeline + hybrid backends; MCP/LangChain/Dify integration; supports Chinese AI accelerators (Ascend, Cambricon, etc.). **MinerU2.5-Pro** (2026) pushes OmniDocBench v1.6 to **95.69**. Code Apache-2.0-derived with high-usage commercial thresholds.

**PaddleOCR-VL (Baidu)** — A 0.9B ultra-compact VLM (NaViT-style dynamic-resolution encoder + ERNIE-4.5-0.3B), **109 languages**, Apache-2.0. OmniDocBench v1.5 overall **92.56** (base), rising through **94.87 (v1.5) / 96.33 (v1.6)** in the 1.5/1.6 updates — a 0.9B model beating Gemini 3 Pro (92.91), GPT-5.2, Qwen3-VL-235B, and MinerU2.5-Pro on v1.6. Runs quantized locally in Ollama/LM Studio. Adds seal recognition and text spotting in 1.5/1.6. This is the strongest *permissively-licensed, small, fully-local* option for highest fidelity.

**dots.ocr (rednote-hilab)** — Single VLM (1.7B LLM base) released July 30, 2025 (arXiv 2512.02498). OmniDocBench text/reading-order SOTA at release; olmOCR-bench 79.1. Introduces XDocParse (126 languages) and excels as a "pure reader." Known weaknesses: complex tables and formula extraction; pictures not parsed (SVG representation still maturing). Later dots.ocr 1.5 reaches ~83.9 on olmOCR-bench.

**olmOCR 2 (Ai2)** — `olmOCR-2-7B-1025` (7B, built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and fine-tuned on olmOCR-mix-1025 / ~270,000 PDF pages), trained with RLVR/GRPO against synthetic unit tests. Per Ai2's launch blog, **"olmOCR 2 scores 82.4 points on our olmOCR-Bench,"** beating marker (76.1) and MinerU (75.8) at release. Fully open (weights, data, training + inference code, permissive licenses). Excellent batch/S3 pipeline for large-scale linearization. Best "reproducible, fully-open" choice for English document data prep at scale.

**marker + Chandra (Datalab)** — marker is a pipeline built around the **Surya** VLM inference server plus small CPU models (pdftext, 20M layout model). **Marker 2** scores olmOCR-bench **76.0 overall / 83.5 born-digital**, at 2.9 pages/s on a B200; a fast/no-OCR CPU path reaches ~23.7 pages/s (43.6 on bench). **Chandra 2** (March 2026) is Datalab's 4B full-page VLM: **olmOCR-bench 85.8** (SOTA at its class), runs on ~10–12GB VRAM. Code Apache-2.0; **weights modified OpenRAIL-M, free only under $2M revenue/funding and "cannot be used competitively with our API."** On the Nanonets leaderboard "Datalab Marker" sits at 83.2 (#2).

**DeepSeek-OCR** — Released Oct 20, 2025 (arXiv 2510.18234). 3B MoE (A570M active) with a "Contexts Optical Compression" DeepEncoder. Per the paper, "when the number of text tokens is within 10 times that of vision tokens (i.e., a compression ratio < 10x), the model can achieve decoding (OCR) precision of 97%… DeepSeek-OCR can generate training data for LLMs/VLMs at a scale of 200k+ pages per day (a single A100-40G)." olmOCR-bench ~75.4. Officially supported in vLLM. **DeepSeek-OCR2** announced Jan 27, 2026. Notable for token efficiency (~100 vision tokens/page) rather than top raw accuracy; interesting for long-context/data-generation use.

**PP-StructureV3 (PaddleOCR pipeline)** — The classic modular pipeline; OmniDocBench v1.5 overall 86.73. Solid CPU/GPU option, superseded in accuracy by PaddleOCR-VL.

**pymupdf4llm** — Lightweight PyMuPDF extension; `to_markdown()`; ~0.01s/page, no ML, no OCR by default. Best default for **born-digital** PDFs feeding RAG. Weak on tables and silently returns near-empty output on scans. **AGPL-3.0** (commercial license needed). A March 2026 "PyMuPDF-Layout" adds a layout model (DocLayNet F1 ~0.864).

**Unstructured** — General ingestion/chunking library with document partitioning; convenient for heterogeneous enterprise formats and RAG, but its parsing fidelity on complex tables/formulas trails the specialized VLMs; often used as an orchestration layer.

**Nougat (Meta)** — 2023 Donut-based academic-PDF→markup model. Still installable (`nougat-ocr`) but **effectively unmaintained** (dependency/transformers breakage, open issues), English/arXiv-only, GPU-recommended. **Superseded** — not recommended for new pipelines.

**Surya** — Datalab's OCR/layout toolkit and the engine inside marker; usable standalone for OCR, layout, reading order, table detection across many languages.

**GOT-OCR2 / Qwen3-VL / InternVL / Nanonets-OCR2/3 / GLM-OCR / HunyuanOCR** — GOT-OCR2 (2024) is now dated (olmOCR-bench 48.3). **Qwen3-VL** (open weights, 2B–235B; 32 languages OCR) is a strong general VLM with good document parsing but is beaten on pure parsing by specialized sub-2B models. **Nanonets-OCR2-3B** (Apache-2.0) adds semantic tagging/LaTeX/handwriting; **Nanonets OCR-3 currently tops the olmOCR-bench leaderboard at 87.4%** (Nanonets also reports 90.5 on OmniDocBench). **GLM-OCR (0.9B, Zhipu)** hit OmniDocBench v1.5 SOTA (94.6). **HunyuanOCR** (Tencent) leads a multilingual parsing benchmark (MORE, 92.42). These are all worth tracking; the field is releasing SOTA models roughly monthly.

### Head-to-head on the requested dimensions

- **Tables:** PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 (TEDS ~92.8), MinerU2.5-Pro (TEDS 93.4), GLM-OCR lead; Docling TableFormer strong for a pipeline; liteparse/pymupdf4llm weak.
- **Math/LaTeX:** MinerU2.5-Pro (CDM 97.3), PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, GLM-OCR lead; Granite-Docling improved (F1 0.968) but small-model ceiling; Nougat historically good but unmaintained; liteparse/pymupdf4llm none.
- **Multi-column / reading order:** VLM parsers (MinerU, PaddleOCR-VL, dots.ocr, olmOCR 2) strong; liteparse "collapses on non-linear layouts."
- **Scanned/OCR quality:** Full-page VLMs (Chandra 2, olmOCR 2, MinerU 2.5) win; note **"Old Scans Math" remains hard for everyone** (top scores <90, most 40–55). pymupdf4llm fails silently on scans.
- **Speed/throughput:** liteparse (~1,721 pg/s no-OCR CPU) > pymupdf4llm (~ms/page) >> marker fast (CPU ~24 pg/s) > VLMs on GPU (MinerU ~2 pg/s A100, marker 2.9 pg/s B200).
- **Hardware/VRAM:** CPU-only viable for pymupdf4llm, liteparse, Docling classic, marker fast/no-OCR, PP-StructureV3. Single consumer GPU (10–16GB) runs Granite-Docling, PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B), MinerU 2.5 (1.2B), dots.ocr, Chandra 2 (~10–12GB). olmOCR 2 (7B) and 3B+ models want ≥16–24GB.
- **Licensing:** Cleanest commercial = MIT (Docling) / Apache-2.0 (PaddleOCR-VL, liteparse, olmOCR, dots.ocr, Granite-Docling, Nanonets-OCR2). Watch OpenRAIL-M caps (marker/Chandra) and AGPL (pymupdf4llm).
- **Languages:** PaddleOCR-VL & MinerU (109), dots.ocr (100+/126 in XDocParse), Chandra 2 (90). Docling/Granite-Docling and olmOCR are English-centric.

## Recommendations

1. **Default self-hosted IDP stack (single GPU):** Use **Docling as the orchestration/normalization layer** (MIT, DoclingDocument, format breadth, RAG integrations) and route the actual page parsing to a **small specialized VLM**. For clean commercial licensing pick **PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B, Apache-2.0)** as the primary parser; use **MinerU 2.5** where you want its mature pipeline+VLM+hybrid tooling and CJK strength. Both fit a 12–16GB consumer GPU.

2. **Highest fidelity on complex tables/formulas:** Deploy **PaddleOCR-VL-1.6** or **MinerU2.5-Pro** (OmniDocBench v1.6 ≥95%), or **GLM-OCR**. Reserve a **Chandra 2** path for the hardest English pages if you clear the OpenRAIL-M revenue threshold; otherwise stay on the Apache-2.0 models.

3. **CPU-only / no-GPU:** For born-digital PDFs, start with **pymupdf4llm** (mind AGPL) or **liteparse** (Apache-2.0, faster and permissive) for text/throughput, and **Docling's classic pipeline** (with TableFormer FAST + Tesseract/RapidOCR) when you need tables and structure without a GPU. Accept lower fidelity on scans and math.

4. **High-throughput batch linearization (GPU):** Use **olmOCR 2** (fully open, S3/batch pipeline, olmOCR-bench 82.4) or **marker** (concurrency model over a shared Surya server) for English corpora; use **liteparse/pymupdf4llm** as a cheap CPU pre-filter to skip pages that are already clean digital text before spending GPU on hard pages.

5. **Architecture pattern — tiered routing:** Classify each page and route: digital-text → liteparse/pymupdf4llm; structured/tabular → Docling+TableFormer or a small VLM; scanned/complex/math → full-page VLM. This mirrors what marker (device-dependent modes) and MinerU (pipeline/VLM/hybrid) now do internally and gives the best cost/quality tradeoff.

**Thresholds that would change these picks:** If your documents are majority scanned or handwritten, weight olmOCR-bench "Old Scans"/Chandra 2 over OmniDocBench and prefer full-page VLMs. If CJK/multilingual dominates, prefer PaddleOCR-VL or MinerU. If you cannot accept any non-permissive weight license, exclude marker/Chandra and pymupdf4llm and standardize on Apache/MIT (PaddleOCR-VL + Docling + olmOCR). If GPU is unavailable, fidelity on tables/math will be materially lower — budget for a GPU if complex documents are core.

## Caveats
- **Benchmarks are saturating and gameable.** OmniDocBench relies on formatting-sensitive edit distance and is English/Chinese-centric; olmOCR-bench's header/footer category can reward *omitting* content. Treat leaderboard deltas of 1–2 points as noise and **run your own corpus** through candidates before committing. Docling's weak OmniDocBench pipeline score partly reflects metric sensitivity, not just extraction failure.
- **Throughput numbers are best-case.** Vendor pages/sec figures (e.g., marker on B200, MinerU on A100) are upper bounds on high-end datacenter GPUs; expect substantially lower on consumer hardware.
- **The field moves monthly.** Between late 2025 and Aug 2026, new SOTA models appeared repeatedly (DeepSeek-OCR/-OCR2, PaddleOCR-VL 1.5/1.6, GLM-OCR, HunyuanOCR, MinerU2.5-Pro, Chandra 2, Nanonets OCR-3). Any "leader" claim has a short shelf life; architect for swappable parser backends.
- **Some secondary sources are unreliable.** One vendor page (idp-software.com) misdated Granite-Docling to "January 2026" and mischaracterized the LF donation; the primary IBM/HF/LF sources confirm Granite-Docling shipped **Sept 17, 2025** and Docling was donated to **LF AI & Data in April 2025**.
- **Licensing nuance matters for production.** OpenRAIL-M "no competitive use" and revenue caps (marker/Chandra) and AGPL (pymupdf4llm) can be blockers; verify current license terms directly before deploying.
